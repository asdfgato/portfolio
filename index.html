LEADERSHIP · Jan 2021

THE THREE PILLARS OF EFFECTIVE LEADERSHIP

▼

Leadership is one of the most challenging yet rewarding soft skills a person can develop. While understanding theory is valuable, real-world application is what truly refines our ability to lead. It requires a high level of introspection and emotional intelligence — being able to step back, recognize successes and failures, and continuously improve.

A strong leader knows when to lead, when to follow, and how to enhance problem-solving within a team. I define leadership through three key pillars:

1. COMMUNICATE CLEARLY

Effective communication is the foundation of strong leadership. It's about striking the right balance in clarity, frequency, and assertiveness. Agile offers structured approaches: daily stand-ups for progress, retrospectives for reflection, sprint reviews to align with stakeholders. Feedback mechanisms like 360° reviews and one-on-ones create valuable introspection opportunities.

One of my preferred leadership models is Radical Candor — challenging directly (sharing honest feedback without arrogance) while caring personally (bringing humanity into professional interactions). Balancing both allows transparent discussions that tackle the elephant in the room without eroding morale.

2. MOTIVATE THE TEAM

"If you want to go fast, go alone. If you want to go far, go together." — Cory Booker

Great leadership fosters collaboration, trust, and accountability. Show genuine interest in team members' growth. Cultivate psychological safety where ideas and concerns can be shared openly. Direct competition toward external goals rather than internal conflicts. A leader's job is not to micromanage — it's to trust the team, guide them, and create space for growth.

3. DELIVER RESULTS

Leadership is ultimately about outcomes. Align on clear goals, adapt when plans change, maintain accountability without stifling creativity. Agile supports results-driven work through iteration and continuous improvement. The key is balancing structure with flexibility.

"To improve is to change; to be perfect is to change often." — Winston Churchill

CREATIVITY · Jan 2021

ON FOSTERING CREATIVITY IN PRODUCT TEAMS

▼

I believe creativity is one of the most valuable skills to cultivate — it integrates curiosity, flexibility, and design thinking to generate value from seemingly insignificant ideas. My studies in Canada reinforced that creativity is built on hard work, smart work, courage, and a bit of luck. It thrives in environments that encourage diversity, open communication, and a culture of empathy.

BARRIERS TO CREATIVITY

Creativity is hindered when there is only one "right" answer, when logic is overemphasized and dismisses unconventional solutions, or when rules are too rigid to allow exploration. A team that fosters creativity must embrace mistakes as an essential part of the iterative process.

HOW TO CULTIVATE IT

  • Foster genuine interest in the problem at hand
  • Ensure creative contributions are valued by leadership
  • Maintain childlike curiosity — approach problems with fresh eyes
  • Produce many ideas and then filter them effectively (Gary Hamel)

SOFT SKILLS TO BUILD IN YOUR TEAM

  • Creative thinking and design reasoning
  • Clear communication and active listening
  • Decision-making and evaluation
  • Time management and prioritization
  • Negotiation and leadership under pressure

Ultimately, a worthwhile goal is to create something truly original that endures. And that starts with making it safe to fail.

UX · Jan 2021

UX DESIGN PRINCIPLES I APPLY TO EVERY PRODUCT

▼

As digital technology evolves, so do our expectations of digital interactions. UX design focuses on crafting the "feel" of a product — ensuring interactions are intuitive, seamless, and meaningful. A UX designer's primary goal is to create products that provide value to users while maximizing overall impact.

WHAT MAKES A PRODUCT WORK

  • Useful — Simple, easy to use, designed with familiarity in mind
  • Usable — Addressing a real need in the user's life
  • Desirable — Visually appealing and capable of evoking positive emotions

HOW I APPLY THIS AS PM

Every product decision I make runs through these three filters. A feature that's technically sound but not useful to the user is waste. A flow that's usable but not desirable will lose to a competitor that cares about craft. UX is not a phase — it's a continuous lens applied from discovery through delivery.

At Elektra we delivered 900+ wireframes and 500+ interface screens in 3 months — only because we had clear UX principles guiding prioritization at every sprint.
